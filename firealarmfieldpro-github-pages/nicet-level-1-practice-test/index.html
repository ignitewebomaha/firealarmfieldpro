FIVE PRACTICE QUESTIONS

A supervised conventional initiating circuit is normal. During an authorized test, one conductor is opened between the panel and its remote end-of-line resistor. What condition should the panel indicate?

  1. An alarm from the initiating zone
  2. A trouble from the initiating zone
  3. A supervisory from the initiating zone
  4. A disabled status for the initiating zone

A sprinkler valve supervisory switch operates while a waterflow switch remains normal. How should the valve condition normally be classified?

  1. A fire alarm requiring evacuation
  2. A circuit trouble requiring repair
  3. A supervisory requiring investigation
  4. A monitor-only event requiring logging

A technician replaces a smoke detector head on an addressable base. The panel now reports a missing device at that address. Which check most directly tests whether the replacement is suitable?

  1. Compare its sensitivity range with the original head
  2. Compare its current rating with the original head
  3. Compare its base dimensions with the original head
  4. Verify its model and protocol against panel compatibility

A conventional circuit has the correct end-of-line resistor connected across the panel terminals instead of at the last device. Which problem does this create?

  1. An open field wire can leave the panel indicating normal
  2. An alarm contact can no longer change the circuit current
  3. The circuit becomes a two-path return arrangement
  4. The resistor causes all connected devices to latch

A room layout requires at least 34 cd. The proposed wall strobe has selectable settings of 15, 30, 75, and 110 cd. Which is the lowest setting that meets the stated output requirement?

  1. 30 cd
  2. 34 cd
  3. 75 cd
  4. 110 cd

LEVEL I SAMPLE REVIEW

Review circuit behavior, not just device names

Use this Level 1 (Level I) practice sample to check your reasoning on the topics below. These are sample review topics, not a complete official exam outline.

Separate alarm, supervisory, and trouble

Practice identifying the condition being reported. A fault in a wiring path is a different event from operation of a sprinkler valve supervisory switch or an alarm-initiating device. Read the scenario for what changed before choosing the classification.

Trace the supervision path

Sketch where the end-of-line component belongs and what the panel can actually monitor. Ask what happens if a conductor opens on either side of that component. Physical device count alone does not tell you whether the wiring is supervised.

Check equipment identification

Match the panel, detector, base, and protocol using the applicable documentation. Devices that fit together mechanically are not automatically compatible. Use model identification as part of the answer, not appearance alone.

For a complete preparation plan, compare your review with NICET’s official Level I content outline. Use the references permitted for your scheduled exam.

Make the review count

Device identification, drawings, wiring fundamentals, and safe field work. Use each missed question to choose a subject for deeper review in your references. These scenarios include field judgment and calculation review; a short sample cannot assess certification readiness.
