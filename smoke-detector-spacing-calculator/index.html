How the calculation works

Columns = ceil(length ÷ spacing); rows = ceil(width ÷ spacing)

Worked example

A 60 × 40 ft rectangle with a selected 30 ft interval produces a 2 × 2 grid. Centering each cell gives 15 ft length offsets and 10 ft width offsets.

What this estimate covers

This is a geometric planning aid, not a code-compliant device count. It assumes a flat, unobstructed rectangular room and does not evaluate beams, slopes, airflow, obstructions, partitions, or detector listing. Review maximum point-to-detector distance and all applicable criteria separately.

Common questions

Is dividing floor area by 900 square feet enough?

Area division alone does not establish a layout. Room shape, wall offsets, distance to the farthest points, and ceiling conditions matter. This tool divides length and width separately to show a preliminary centered grid.

Does a 30 ft spacing input mean the layout complies with NFPA 72?

No. Thirty feet is an example input. Verify the spacing and method applicable to the actual detector, adopted edition, and ceiling. The tool does not evaluate beams, sloped ceilings, airflow, or obstructions.

What do the first center offsets represent?

The room is divided into equal grid cells. Each preliminary detector sits at a cell center, so its first offset from each wall is half the corresponding cell dimension. Check the completed layout against all applicable distance criteria.
